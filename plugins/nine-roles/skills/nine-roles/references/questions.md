# Questions that change the meeting

Two kinds of question reach the owner. One asks for a number they already have.
The other asks something they have never said out loud, and the room goes quiet
while they answer it.

A stop needs both. Three questions maximum, and at least one from this file.

## The rule that keeps this honest

**Before you ask, write down what changes with each plausible answer.**

If the meeting does the same thing whatever they say, you are not asking a
question. You are making conversation, and you are spending the owner's
attention on nothing.

Write it like this, in your own head, not on the page:

> "If they say the money is idle, the cash argument dies and delivery stops
> competing with the branch. If they say it is borrowed, the payback line
> becomes the whole decision."

Then ask.

## What makes a question land

- It asks about something that happened, not something they intend. "What did
  you do the last time a supplier let you down?" beats "how would you handle a
  supplier problem?"
- It is answerable in two sentences, and not answerable without thinking.
- The owner has the answer. Nobody else in the room does. That is why it goes
  to them and not to the Analyst.
- No yes or no, unless yes and no produce two different meetings.
- No trap. No question you would ignore the answer to. No question you ask to
  make a point you have already decided.
- One question per sentence. A question with an "and" in the middle gets half
  an answer.

## The families

Pick one family per stop. Never two questions from the same family.

---

### 1. What the decision is actually for

The stated question is rarely the real one. A second branch can mean growth,
boredom, a competitor's new shop, or a son who needs a job.

- If we say no to this today, what do you do with the money instead?
  «اگر امروز بگوییم نه، آن پول را کجا می‌گذارید؟»
- Why now? What changed in the last three months that put this on the table?
  «چرا حالا؟ سه ماه اخیر چه چیزی عوض شد که این موضوع آمد روی میز؟»
- Who first suggested this?
  «اولین بار چه کسی این را پیشنهاد داد؟»
- If a competitor did this tomorrow and you did nothing, what would you lose?
  «اگر فردا رقیب این کار را بکند و شما هیچ نکنید، چه چیزی از دست می‌دهید؟»

**What it changes.** The answer decides which option set is on the table. A
decision made to keep up with a competitor gets different options from one made
to use idle capacity.

---

### 2. What success looks like on a real day

Owners describe success in adjectives. Ask for a Tuesday.

- Two years from now this worked. Describe the Tuesday. How many people are
  working, where are you standing, what are you doing at eleven in the morning?
  «دو سال دیگر این گرفته. یک سه‌شنبه‌اش را برایم بگویید. چند نفر سر کارند،
  خودتان کجا ایستاده‌اید، ساعت یازده صبح دارید چه کار می‌کنید؟»
- What number would make you say it worked, and when do you want to see it?
  «چه عددی به شما می‌گوید گرفت؟ کِی می‌خواهید آن عدد را ببینید؟»
- Do you want to run two of these? Not can you. Want to.
  «شما دلتان می‌خواهد دو تای این را بگردانید؟ نمی‌پرسم می‌توانید. می‌پرسم
  دلتان می‌خواهد؟»

**What it changes.** The review date and the definition of done come from this
answer. An owner who does not want to run two branches has just told you which
option to build.

---

### 3. What would make you stop

Ask for the exit before anyone is attached to the plan.

- What would you have to see, in month three, to close this?
  «تا ماه سوم چه چیزی ببینید که جمعش کنید؟»
- Write the number now: below what figure do we stop?
  «همین حالا عدد را بگویید: زیر چه رقمی می‌ایستیم؟»
- Last time something did not work, how long did you keep it running?
  «دفعه‌ی قبل که چیزی جواب نداد، چقدر نگهش داشتید؟»

**What it changes.** The owner's own number becomes the trigger in the
pessimistic scenario. A trigger the owner set is one the owner obeys.

---

### 4. What the delay is costing

Deciding slowly is a decision, and it has a price.

- What has this cost you every month you have not decided?
  «هر ماه که تصمیم نگرفته‌اید، چقدر برایتان خرج برداشته؟»
- How long has this been on your mind?
  «چند وقت است این موضوع در ذهنتان است؟»
- What happens if we decide nothing today?
  «اگر امروز هیچ تصمیمی نگیریم، چه می‌شود؟»

**What it changes.** It sets the timebox. A cost of nothing per month buys
three weeks of measurement. A cost of twenty million a month does not.

---

### 5. What you cannot take back

Separate the reversible from the locked.

- If this fails in month four, what can you sell, and what is simply gone?
  «اگر ماه چهارم شکست بخورد، چه چیزی را می‌شود فروخت و چه چیزی دود می‌شود؟»
- How long is the lease, and what does breaking it cost?
  «قرارداد چند ماهه است؟ فسخش چقدر آب می‌خورد؟»
- If you hire four people and it does not work, what do you do with them?
  «چهار نفر استخدام می‌کنید و نمی‌گیرد. با آن‌ها چه می‌کنید؟»

**What it changes.** A reversible plan gets tested. A locked one gets measured
first. This question alone reorders the whole meeting.

---

### 6. Who loses if this works

Every plan takes something from somebody inside the company.

- If this succeeds, whose job gets harder?
  «اگر این بگیرد، کار چه کسی سخت‌تر می‌شود؟»
- Who in the company thinks this is a bad idea, and what do they say?
  «چه کسی در مجموعه این را بد می‌داند؟ حرفش چیست؟»
- Who has to change how they work on Monday morning?
  «دوشنبه صبح چه کسی باید روش کارش را عوض کند؟»

**What it changes.** It produces the blocker nobody writes down: the person who
will slow it without saying no.

---

### 7. What you have already promised

Half of the constraints in a room are commitments nobody mentioned.

- Have you told anyone this is happening?
  «به کسی گفته‌اید که این کار انجام می‌شود؟»
- Is there a promise here to a family member, a partner, or a landlord?
  «قولی به کسی داده‌اید؟ شریک، خانواده، صاحب‌ملک؟»
- What did you already pay for?
  «تا حالا پول چه چیزی را داده‌اید؟»

**What it changes.** A promise already made is a constraint, and hiding it
makes the meeting solve the wrong problem.

---

### 8. What would change your mind

The sharpest question in the file. Keep it for the moment the room is
converging.

- What would have to be true for you to change your mind about this?
  «چه چیزی باید درست باشد که نظرتان عوض شود؟»
- If our numbers came back against it, would you still do it?
  «اگر عددهای ما خلافش را نشان بدهد، باز هم انجامش می‌دهید؟»

**What it changes.** If nothing would change their mind, the meeting is not
deciding, it is approving. Say that out loud, kindly, and switch the meeting to
"how do we make this work" instead of "should we do this". Both are useful
meetings. Running the second while everyone thinks it is the first wastes the
day.

---

### 9. What the last time taught you

- When did you last open something new? What surprised you?
  «آخرین بار چه چیزی راه انداختید؟ چه چیزی غافلگیرتان کرد؟»
- What did the first month actually look like, compared to what you expected?
  «ماه اولش با آنچه فکر می‌کردید چقدر فرق داشت؟»
- Which of your current costs did you not see coming when you started?
  «کدام هزینه‌ی امروزتان را اول کار پیش‌بینی نکرده بودید؟»

**What it changes.** It calibrates the Analyst's assumptions with this owner's
own history, which beats an industry average.

---

### 10. The quiet one

Ask once, near the end, and then be silent.

- Is there anything about this you have not said out loud?
  «چیزی هست که درباره‌ی این موضوع نگفته باشید؟»

**What it changes.** Sometimes nothing. Sometimes the whole meeting.

---

## How to deliver a deep question

The Coordinator asks, and names who wanted to know:

> **شیرین (هماهنگ‌کننده):** بهرام یک سؤال دارد که من هم جوابش را می‌خواهم.
> اگر شعبه را نزنید، آن ۷۷۰ میلیون کجا می‌رود؟

Then stop. Do not soften it, do not offer a menu of answers, do not fill the
silence with an example. A question followed by three suggested answers is not
a question any more.

When the answer comes back, say out loud what it changed:

> **مریم (تحلیل‌گر):** پس پول بیکار است. آن‌وقت هزینه‌ی فرصت از تحلیل حذف
> می‌شود و می‌ماند ریسک قفل‌شدن. عدد سربه‌سر عوض نمی‌شود، ولی وزنش عوض شد.

If the owner says "I don't know", that is an answer and it is often the most
useful one in the meeting. Write it in the minutes as an open question with a
date, and carry on.
