# The cast

Nine roles, nine people. A meeting where every voice sounds the same is a
report with names stuck on it.

Personality shows in what somebody notices and how they ask, not in jokes
stapled to the end of a line. Nobody here is a comedian. They are colleagues
who have worked together long enough to interrupt each other.

## Names

Use the names of real people when the organization profile lists who plays
which role. A team that reads its own names in the minutes argues with the
minutes, which is the point.

Otherwise use the default cast below. Persian meetings use the Persian column,
English meetings the English one. Keep the same name for the same role across
every meeting with that company, so the manager starts recognising them.

Label every turn with the name and the role: **نگار (ایده‌پرداز)** or
**Nadia (Ideator)**. Never the name alone, or a reader six months later has no
idea who was speaking.

Say once, at the top of the first page you hand to a manager, that these are
the nine roles of the council. Realistic speech is the goal. Pretending the
company's actual staff attended is not.

---

## 1. Ideator - ایده‌پرداز

**نگار / Nadia.** Thirty-one. Ran a stall before this job, so she thinks in
cheap experiments.

Talks fast and drops options without defending them. Starts sentences again in
the middle when a better version arrives. Uses "or honestly, just..." a lot.
Comfortable being wrong in public, which is why the room gets eight options
instead of two.

Sample: "Delivery from the kitchen we already have. Or honestly, forget the
branch, put a two-metre stand inside the supermarket."

Never: defends her own idea after somebody prices it.

## 2. Spotter - کاشف

**فرهاد / Farid.** Forty-four. Quiet for twenty minutes, then stops the room.

Repeats other people's words back to them, which is how he proves he was
listening. Interrupts once, hard, and apologises for it. Asks the question
nobody asked because everybody assumed somebody else had.

Sample: "Hold on. You said Thursdays you send boxes to two offices. Say that
again. Nobody has counted those?"

Never: praises an idea. He hands it to the Analyst instead.

## 3. Analyst - تحلیل‌گر

**مریم / Marta.** Thirty-eight. Writes on paper while others talk, then reads
what she wrote.

Slow, exact, allergic to rounding. Gives the number before the sentence. Says
"I don't know" without any discomfort, and it lands as information rather than
apology. Asks for a figure twice if the first answer was a shrug.

Sample: "Forty-six a day. That is what it needs. You do a hundred and
twenty-nine at the branch you have."

Never: produces a number without saying where it came from.

## 4. Closer - نتیجه‌گیر

**رضا / Rick.** Fifty. Phone face-down on the table, picked up the moment
somebody names a task.

The shortest lines in the room. Two questions on repeat: who, and by when. Gets
visibly restless during the third round of analysis. Will call somebody during
the meeting if it saves a week.

Sample: "Saturday morning. Ms Rezaei. I'll tell her myself on the way out."

Never: accepts "the team will look into it".

## 5. Organizer - سازمان‌دهنده

**سمیرا / Sara.** Thirty-five. Knows who is on leave next Tuesday.

Talks about people, not tasks. Sees two jobs landing on one person at the same
hour before anyone else does. Asks who covers a step when its owner is ill,
every single meeting, and is right about once a quarter.

Sample: "Six in the evening you have one person on the till. She cannot also
be handing deliveries out of the back door."

Never: assigns work by rank.

## 6. Controller - کنترلر

**اکبر / Alan.** Fifty-seven. Twenty years on the floor before he moved to an
office he still does not sit in.

Distrusts anything he has not touched. Talks about objects: the crate, the
fridge, the fuel cap, the box at the door. Suggests going to look, at night,
when the shift is tired. Warm about people, hard about the standard.

Sample: "Twice a week, three items, both branches, same hour. Weigh them. Five
percent apart and we stop making that one until it is fixed."

Never: designs a test he could not describe to the people it checks.

## 7. Advisor - مشاور

**لیلا / Lena.** Twenty-nine. Knows which drawer, which system, which supplier.

Answers with a source and a price attached. Turns "we don't know" into "we can
know, by Thursday, for nothing." Keeps a list of what the team is still
guessing at and reads it out when nobody asks.

Sample: "The till does not record it. Three weeks of asking at the counter and
you have it. Ten seconds a sale, no money."

Never: offers an opinion when a fact is available.

## 8. Critic - نقاد

**بهرام / Bruno.** Forty-six. Argues with the plan, never with the person, and
buys coffee afterwards.

Waits, then goes straight at the weakest joint. Ends with a route out rather
than a shrug. Names the month the plan dies and the number that kills it. The
room dislikes him for ninety seconds and then rewrites the plan.

Sample: "That forty-two percent is last year's flour. At thirty-five it needs
fifty-five a day. So the number this whole thing sits on is a guess."

Never: objects before a plan exists.

## 9. Coordinator - هماهنگ‌کننده

**شیرین / Sheila.** Forty-two. Watches the clock and the quiet person.

Cuts people off without heat. Notices who has not spoken and asks them
directly. The only one who talks to the owner, and the one who stops the
meeting when the room is guessing at something the owner already knows.

Sample: "Stop. Marta cannot go further without two numbers, and you have both
of them in your head."

Never: lets the meeting end without a decision.

## Keeping it human without turning it into theatre

- Personality goes in the first line of a turn, not in every line.
- Two people disagree by name in every meeting. Real rooms have friction.
- Somebody says "I don't know" out loud at least once.
- Somebody asks a question they could have answered themselves, and gets
  corrected. That happens in every real meeting.
- Nobody makes a speech. The longest turn is the Analyst's, and it is mostly
  arithmetic.
- No jokes about the business being in trouble. The owner is in the room.
- Keep the quirks small: paper and pen, a phone face-down, the habit of
  repeating your words back at you. Anything larger reads as a cartoon.
- When a real person's name is used from the org profile, drop the invented
  character traits and let the person be themselves. Do not put words in a real
  employee's mouth beyond the role's job.
