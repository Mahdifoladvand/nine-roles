# Sector notes

Read the section for the organization's sector before the Analyst and the
Controller speak. Each note carries the same four lines:

- **Inspect** - what the Controller actually looks at, physically.
- **The number that lies** - the figure that is most often quoted and least
  often true in this sector.
- **The hidden cost** - the money that does not appear in the plan people write.
- **Check the law** - what needs a permit, a licence or an inspection. Name it;
  do not rule on it. You are not the lawyer.

If the sector is not listed, use the closest one and say which you used.

---

## Food production and FMCG

- **Inspect.** The product on the shelf, in the shop, at the end of the route,
  at nine at night. Temperature, date codes, facing, whether it is in the
  fridge or standing in the sun. The returns crate tells you more than the
  sales report.
- **The number that lies.** Sell-in. Product shipped to a distributor is not
  product sold to a person. Ask for sell-out or count the shelf yourself.
- **The hidden cost.** Returns, breakage, expiry, and the cash tied up in a
  distributor's payment terms.
- **Check the law.** Production licence, health certificates, labelling rules,
  cold-chain requirements.

## Restaurants, cafes and hospitality

- **Inspect.** Waiting time at the door, the plate leaving the pass, the
  toilets, the temperature of what arrives at the table, and the state of the
  place forty minutes before closing.
- **The number that lies.** Covers. A full room at the wrong average spend
  loses money faster than an empty one. Watch the margin per seat-hour.
- **The hidden cost.** Waste, staff turnover, and the slow days that the good
  days are quietly paying for.
- **Check the law.** Health inspection, food-handling certificates, alcohol and
  music licences where they apply, fire exits.

## Retail

- **Inspect.** Stock accuracy against the system, the shelf at closing time,
  the queue at the till, what customers pick up and put back.
- **The number that lies.** Footfall. Count conversion - people who bought
  divided by people who entered - or the footfall number is decoration.
- **The hidden cost.** Shrinkage, dead stock, and the shelf space a slow
  product occupies while a fast one is out of stock.
- **Check the law.** Trading licence, price display rules, returns and consumer
  guarantee obligations.

## Manufacturing and workshops

- **Inspect.** Scrap rate at each station, machine downtime, the work-in-
  progress sitting between stations, and whether material issued matches
  material recorded.
- **The number that lies.** Capacity. Nameplate capacity assumes no changeover,
  no breakdown and no absence. Measure output on a real week.
- **The hidden cost.** Changeover time, rework, and stock held because the
  supplier is unreliable.
- **Check the law.** Workplace safety, machine guarding, waste disposal,
  environmental permits.

## Software, SaaS and digital products

- **Inspect.** What a new user actually does in the first ten minutes, error
  rates in the log, the support inbox, and how long a fix takes to reach the
  customer.
- **The number that lies.** Registrations. Count people who came back in week
  two. Everything before that is curiosity.
- **The hidden cost.** Maintenance of what you already built, and the support
  load that grows with every feature.
- **Check the law.** Data protection, where personal data is stored, payment
  handling, and what you promise in the terms.

## Clinics, pharmacies and health services

- **Inspect.** Waiting time, appointment adherence, record completeness, stock
  of what expires, and hygiene at the point of care.
- **The number that lies.** Visits. Return rate and outcome matter; volume
  alone can mean the same problem coming back.
- **The hidden cost.** Idle practitioner time, no-shows, and insurance
  reimbursement delay.
- **Check the law.** Practice licences, professional registration, patient
  record confidentiality, advertising restrictions on medical claims. Clinical
  decisions are never for this council.

## Education and training

- **Inspect.** Attendance across the whole course, not week one. Completion.
  What the learner can do afterwards that they could not do before.
- **The number that lies.** Enrolment. Completion and repeat purchase are the
  real figures.
- **The hidden cost.** Preparation time, and refunds after the second session.
- **Check the law.** Teaching permits, certification claims, and what you may
  advertise as a qualification.

## Agencies and professional services

- **Inspect.** Hours actually worked against hours billed, revision rounds per
  project, and how long an invoice takes to be paid.
- **The number that lies.** The day rate. Compute the realised rate: fee
  divided by hours truly spent, including revisions and meetings.
- **The hidden cost.** Scope creep, unpaid pitching, and the client who pays
  late every time and is kept anyway.
- **Check the law.** Contracts, intellectual property ownership, and
  confidentiality obligations to clients.

## Logistics and distribution

- **Inspect.** The route at the end of the day, delivery-window adherence,
  vehicle load factor, and the condition of goods on arrival.
- **The number that lies.** Deliveries per day. Cost per successful delivery,
  after failed attempts and returns, is the real one.
- **The hidden cost.** Empty running, waiting time at the customer, and fuel
  when the price moves.
- **Check the law.** Vehicle permits, driver hours, insurance, and rules for
  what may be carried.

## Non-profit and public bodies

- **Inspect.** What reached the intended person, not what was dispatched. The
  gap between the two is the whole programme.
- **The number that lies.** Money raised or budget spent. Neither is an outcome.
- **The hidden cost.** Reporting burden, and the volunteer or staff time that
  never appears in the budget.
- **Check the law.** Procurement rules, reporting obligations, restrictions on
  the use of restricted funds.

---

## Two questions that apply to every sector

**Where does the customer first see it go wrong?** Whatever that is, the
Controller inspects it first, and inspects it where it happens, not from a
desk.

**What number does this sector habitually flatter itself with?** Every industry
has one. Find it, refuse it, and replace it with the number that survives a bad
month.
